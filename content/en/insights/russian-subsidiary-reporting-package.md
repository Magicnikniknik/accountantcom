A Russian subsidiary may close its statutory books on one timetable while group finance expects a separate reporting pack on another. The weak solution is to rebuild the numbers manually each month. The stronger solution is a repeatable bridge between the local ledger and headquarters reporting.

## Start with a mapping that has an owner

Document how local accounts map to group reporting lines. Where one local account feeds several group lines, define the allocation logic and evidence required. Version the mapping when the chart of accounts or reporting package changes.

## Separate source data from transformation entries

The reporting file should distinguish local-book values from group adjustments. Each adjustment needs a description, calculation, period, preparer, reviewer and support. This keeps the statutory ledger intact while making the group layer reproducible.

## Reconcile intercompany before submission

For each group counterparty, compare the balance, currency, transaction period and nature of the difference. Differences should not be hidden inside generic top-side adjustments. Assign an owner and resolution date.

## Explain movements, not only balances

Headquarters usually needs to understand why the result changed. A useful commentary separates operational movement from timing, foreign exchange, reclassification and non-recurring items, with a link to the supporting schedule.

## Package the open issues

Do not wait for every question to disappear. A controlled issue register should show the amount, affected reporting line, available evidence, provisional treatment, responsible person and expected resolution date.
