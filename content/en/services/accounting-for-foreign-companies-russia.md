Foreign-owned companies operating in Russia often need one finance process to satisfy several audiences at once: local accounting and tax requirements, headquarters reporting, group consolidation and internal controls.

Accountant & Co. supports foreign businesses with a structured accounting workflow designed to keep local records, reporting packages and management information aligned.

## What we support

- bookkeeping and period-end close coordination;
- local accounting and tax reporting workflow;
- primary-document and ledger review;
- reconciliations of bank, supplier, customer and intercompany balances;
- management reporting for overseas headquarters;
- preparation of schedules and evidence for audit;
- coordination of accounting questions between the Russian operation and group finance teams.

## A single reporting process instead of parallel spreadsheets

A common problem in cross-border operations is that local accounting, headquarters reporting and tax work evolve as separate processes. The result is duplicate adjustments, unexplained differences and a difficult year-end close.

We structure the reporting chain so that source documents, local ledgers, adjustments and management reports can be traced back to the underlying transaction. Where the group uses IFRS or another reporting framework, the local close can be connected to a separate reconciliation and transformation layer rather than replacing the statutory records.

## Who this service is for

This service may be relevant to:

- foreign-owned Russian legal entities;
- international groups with a Russian subsidiary;
- companies coordinating finance remotely from another jurisdiction;
- businesses that need local bookkeeping plus group reporting;
- finance teams preparing for audit, due diligence or a change of accounting provider.

## Tax registration and company structure matter

The accounting and tax workflow depends on the legal form, tax status, activities and way the foreign business operates in Russia. The Federal Tax Service maintains specific registration procedures for foreign organisations, so the first step is to understand the actual operating structure rather than apply a generic template.

## Typical onboarding

1. Review the legal and reporting structure.
2. Collect the current ledger, tax registers and key reconciliations.
3. Identify missing documents, open balances and reporting differences.
4. Agree the monthly close calendar and responsibilities.
5. Build recurring local and headquarters reporting packages.
6. Maintain an issues log for material accounting and tax questions.
