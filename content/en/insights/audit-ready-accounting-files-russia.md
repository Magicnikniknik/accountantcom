Financial review, due diligence and external audit all become slower when supporting evidence is scattered across email, personal folders and undocumented spreadsheets. Review readiness is an operating discipline rather than a one-time data-room exercise.

## Create a period evidence index

For each reporting period, maintain a consistent structure for the locked ledger, reconciliations, material contracts, manual-journal support, estimates, tax schedules, intercompany confirmations and management approvals.

## Make every material manual entry explainable

A manual journal should state the business purpose, accounts, amount, calculation, source documents, preparer and reviewer. Entries that cannot be reconstructed later create disproportionate review effort.

## Retain the logic behind estimates

Where the accounts contain estimates or judgments, preserve the assumptions, source data, calculation method, review evidence and subsequent changes. The objective is not to eliminate judgment but to make it inspectable.

## Keep an exceptions register

Open questions, missing documents and unreconciled balances should be visible throughout the year. Assign an amount, owner, deadline and potential reporting effect rather than hiding the item in a general cleanup list.
