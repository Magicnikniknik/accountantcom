A monthly close is not a calendar date and it is not a single spreadsheet. It is a controlled evidence package showing how source transactions became the balances reported to management, tax specialists and headquarters.

The key test is simple: **can another competent reviewer reproduce every material balance without reconstructing the month from email?** If not, the close may be finished administratively but it is not reviewable.

## The five layers of the close package

### 1. Close control sheet

The control sheet identifies the entity, period, reporting framework, close version and responsible preparer/reviewer. It lists each close task, owner, deadline, completion status and link to evidence. This is the index of the package, not a decorative checklist.

### 2. Locked source numbers

Retain the trial balance, general-ledger extract and key subledger outputs used for reporting. If the ledger changes after the package is issued, the change should be visible through a new version or post-close adjustment log. Otherwise the reporting pack and source ledger quietly diverge.

### 3. Balance-sheet reconciliations

At minimum, material balances should have a clear source and reconciling logic. Typical schedules include:

- bank and cash reconciliations;
- receivables and payables ageing;
- supplier and customer advances;
- intercompany balances by counterparty;
- tax balances and payments;
- fixed assets and depreciation;
- inventory where material;
- loans, interest and foreign-exchange movements;
- accruals, provisions and prepayments.

Each schedule should state the ledger balance, supporting balance, difference, explanation and next action. A folder of documents without this bridge is evidence storage, not reconciliation.

### 4. Performance explanations

Management reporting should explain significant movements rather than repeat ledger captions. Useful commentary separates volume, price, foreign exchange, timing, one-off items and accounting reclassifications. The explanation should connect to a schedule or source report.

### 5. Issue and judgment register

Unresolved items do not disappear because the month is closed. Record the amount, affected account, facts known, missing evidence, provisional treatment, owner, deadline and whether the issue could change tax, local reporting or group reporting.

## A practical close sequence

| Stage | Control question | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Cut-off | Are all expected documents received or accrued? | Missing-document list and accrual schedule |
| Posting | Are transactions recorded in the correct period and accounts? | Ledger review and exception list |
| Reconciliation | Do material balances agree to independent support? | Signed reconciliation schedules |
| Review | Were unusual entries and movements challenged? | Review notes and resolved comments |
| Reporting | Does the reporting pack trace to the locked ledger? | Mapping and version record |
| Archive | Can the package be retrieved as issued? | Period folder with controlled naming |

## Materiality keeps the process usable

Not every account needs the same depth. Define thresholds for reconciliation differences, movement commentary and reviewer sign-off. Higher-risk balances — cash, tax, related parties, manual journals and long-outstanding items — may require review even below a general threshold.

The close calendar should also distinguish recurring work from historic cleanup. Mixing old reconstruction into ordinary monthly tasks hides the real cost of both and makes deadlines unreliable.

## The output management should receive

A useful monthly package normally combines the financial statements, cash and working-capital view, material movement explanations, compliance status and issue register. Headquarters may additionally need a group chart-of-accounts mapping, intercompany confirmation and [RAS-to-IFRS transformation layer](/en/services/ras-ifrs-parallel-reporting/).
