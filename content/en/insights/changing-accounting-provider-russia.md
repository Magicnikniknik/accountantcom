Changing an accounting provider is not an email forwarding exercise. The business is transferring control over records, filing history, system access, reporting logic and unresolved risks. A rushed cutover can leave management paying for a new monthly service while nobody owns the old balances.

The transition should be managed as a short project with named deliverables and acceptance criteria.

## Stage 1: define custody and access

Create an access register before the handover date. It should cover accounting systems, electronic document exchange, tax portals, banking exports, payroll tools, reporting portals, shared drives and any credentials controlled by individuals.

Do not put passwords into the handover spreadsheet. Record the system, access owner, business administrator, transfer method, status and test date. Revoke former access only after replacement access has been verified and evidence retained.

## Stage 2: capture the accounting record

Request exports in open, reusable formats as well as native backups where appropriate. The handover package normally includes:

- trial balances and general ledger by relevant period;
- chart of accounts and analytical dimensions;
- tax registers, returns and filing/payment evidence;
- bank, receivable, payable and intercompany reconciliations;
- fixed-asset, inventory, loan, accrual and prepayment schedules;
- payroll summaries if in scope;
- accounting policies and recurring close instructions;
- prior management or group reporting packs;
- audit requests, findings and open issue logs;
- a source-document index and archive location.

File counts are not acceptance. Test whether exports open, totals reconcile and analytical detail is sufficient to reproduce key balances.

## Stage 3: validate opening balances

The new provider should not silently accept every closing number as correct. Prioritize cash, tax, related parties, debt, equity, long-outstanding receivables/payables, manual journals and accounts without reconciliations.

Classify each issue:

| Status | Meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Confirmed | Balance agrees to adequate support | Accept into recurring close |
| Difference | Register and support do not agree | Quantify and assign remediation |
| Missing data | Support is not available | Escalate and assess reporting impact |
| Judgment | Treatment needs professional conclusion | Assign reviewer and deadline |

This classification creates a visible opening-position risk register.

## Stage 4: separate cleanup from monthly service

Historic reconstruction is unpredictable. Put it into a separate backlog with periods, accounts, estimated effort and priority. Otherwise the first months look permanently late and neither management nor provider can distinguish inherited problems from current performance.

The recurring scope should still start with a close calendar, responsibility matrix and agreed [monthly close package](/en/insights/monthly-close-package-russia/).

## Stage 5: run a controlled first close

During the first period, compare outputs from old logic and new logic where possible. Confirm filing deadlines, reporting mappings, sign-offs and exception escalation. At the end, management should receive an accepted opening position, first close package, unresolved-issue register and access inventory.
