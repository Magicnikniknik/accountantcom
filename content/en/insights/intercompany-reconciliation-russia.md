Intercompany reconciliation is not simply comparing two closing balances. A useful reconciliation explains why the balances agree or why they do not.

## Compare at transaction level when needed

Start with the counterparty and closing balance, then drill down by document, invoice or settlement reference where a difference exists. Compare transaction dates, accounting periods, currencies and the nature of the entry on both sides.

## Classify differences consistently

Useful categories include timing, missing document, settlement in transit, foreign-exchange treatment, account mapping, disputed amount and manual adjustment. Consistent categories make recurring failure patterns visible.

## Separate confirmation from resolution

A balance can be confirmed as different. That is not the same as resolving it. The reconciliation should show the confirmed difference, root cause, responsible side, required action and expected clearing period.

## Link the result to group reporting

If the group requires elimination entries, retain a bridge from the local intercompany balances to the group elimination. Do not let the elimination file become a substitute for correcting local accounting when a local error has been identified.
