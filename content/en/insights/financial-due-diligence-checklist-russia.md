A data room can contain thousands of files and still fail to answer the basic deal questions: what the business really earns, how much working capital it needs, what behaves like debt and which reported balances are not supported.

The first request should therefore establish a reconciled financial spine. Detailed documents are added around material questions, not collected without purpose.

## Priority 1: financial spine

Request these items first for the review period and a comparable prior period:

- statutory financial statements and management accounts;
- monthly trial balances and general-ledger extracts;
- mapping between statutory, management and group reporting;
- monthly revenue, gross profit and operating expense detail;
- cash-flow information and bank balance summary;
- budgets, forecasts and explanations of major variances;
- accounting policies and list of material changes.

Totals should reconcile. If management reporting cannot be bridged to the ledger, every later analysis inherits an unmeasured reliability problem.

## Priority 2: quality of earnings

Collect revenue by customer, product/service, entity and month; major contracts; returns or credit notes; one-off income and expenses; related-party items; capitalized costs; and management's EBITDA adjustments.

For every proposed normalization, retain the amount, period, ledger accounts, commercial explanation, supporting document and assessment of whether the item could recur. Labels such as “one-off” are claims, not evidence.

## Priority 3: working capital

Request receivables and payables ageing, inventory movement and obsolescence data, advances, accruals, provisions, deferred income and month-end balance history. Normalize for seasonality and cut-off before selecting a target working-capital level.

Investigate balances that are old, disputed, related-party, offset manually or inconsistent with the underlying contract.

## Priority 4: debt and debt-like items

Include bank facilities, shareholder and intercompany loans, interest, leases, guarantees, overdue taxes, unpaid capex, factoring, supplier financing, litigation-related provisions and other items that may transfer economic value at completion.

The classification should follow the transaction definition agreed by the parties. A due diligence report can identify and quantify items; it should not silently rewrite deal terms.

## Priority 5: tax and compliance interface

Collect material returns, tax-to-ledger reconciliations, tax account balances, correspondence made available for review, audits, disputes and supporting files for material or unusual transactions. Financial reviewers should distinguish observed exceptions from formal tax conclusions and escalate specialist questions.

For current public Russian tax information, the authoritative starting point is the [Federal Tax Service](https://www.nalog.gov.ru/).

## Priority 6: related parties and intercompany

Obtain the legal/entity map, related-party list, contracts, charges, loans, reconciliations and settlement history. Match balances counterparty by counterparty and explain differences in timing, currency, classification and missing invoices.

## Data-room control sheet

Track request number, document owner, period, status, date received, version, reviewer, exceptions and follow-up. Use four evidence labels consistently: **received, reconciled, partially supported, not supported**.

That distinction matters. A file can be received without agreeing to the ledger, and a management explanation can be plausible without being independently supported.

## The deliverable should preserve uncertainty

Separate confirmed findings, estimates, management representations and unresolved gaps. Quantify ranges where possible, state the source and avoid presenting unavailable evidence as a clean conclusion.
