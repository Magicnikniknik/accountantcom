Cross-border finance is vulnerable to failure when the commercial transaction, accounting records, payment documents and bank-facing evidence are prepared by separate teams without a shared control file.

The useful objective is not to promise that a particular route will work. It is to make the transaction package complete, internally consistent and reviewable before it reaches the next control point.

## Document the transaction purpose

Record the parties, commercial purpose, contractual basis, amount, currency, expected settlement date and internal approver. Link the payment request to the accounting entry or planned accounting treatment.

## Maintain one evidence package

Keep the signed contract and relevant supporting documents together with invoices, acceptance evidence, calculations, correspondence on material changes and the final payment instruction. The exact set depends on the transaction.

## Check consistency across documents

Names, amounts, currency, payment purpose and contractual references should not conflict across the payment instruction and supporting evidence. Where a difference is legitimate, document the explanation before submission.

## Track external requirements separately

Bank procedures and regulatory requirements can change. Record which current external requirement was checked, by whom and when. Do not hard-code an old checklist into a permanent process.

For related support, see [Cross-Border Finance](/en/services/cross-border-finance/).

Current requirements should be verified against the actual transaction and the rules and bank procedures in force at the time, including applicable official information from the Bank of Russia and other competent authorities.
